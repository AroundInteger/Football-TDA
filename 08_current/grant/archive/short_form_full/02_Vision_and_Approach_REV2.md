## Vision

### 1. Research Problem and Mathematical Contribution

This project builds the statistical-topology framework that competitive collective systems currently lack, and proves the two theorems that make it usable. These are spatially distributed multi-agent systems in a bounded domain, in which agents coordinate internally while responding to an active adversary — autonomous multi-agent coordination, biological collectives, tactical team sports. Statistical topology can already summarise spatial organisation, but only for cooperative collectives or where observations can be treated as independent. No framework yet quantifies how large-scale organisation forms, changes and breaks down under competition. This grant is a proof of principle: it proves the theorems and validates them at scale on a fully tracked benchmark system (§2), producing the evidence base for a follow-on Standard Grant.

**Importance.** Two obstacles limit existing approaches.

*Scale.* Organisation exists at several spatial scales at once — small interaction groups, larger formations, enclosed coverage regions. Persistent homology is multi-scale in its filtration parameter, but a single filtration over the full agent set does not separate these levels: features from different levels interleave in one diagram [1–3]. Multiparameter persistence [4,5] is a principled route but is computationally impractical at the required data rates.

*Dependence.* Each agent continuously adapts to its opponents. This violates the exchangeability assumptions underpinning current statistical topology [6,8] and invalidates inference treating successive observations as independent.

**Mathematical contribution.** The project targets two theorems (§5).

**(T1) Averaging under competitive dependence is well posed.** The empirical mean path of landscape summaries converges under temporal mixing rather than exchangeability, and the limiting covariance is the long-run, not the marginal, covariance [6,24,25].

**(T2) Transitions are localised with a proven error bound.** The error is set by the size of the change, T1's long-run variance, and the worst-case perturbation of the input diagrams [7,19,26,27].

Both are explicit, checkable claims with named failure conditions. O1 establishes the population-scale geometry they rest on; O2 delivers the proofs and validates them at season scale (§4). If the landscape argument is intractable, the R3 fallback (§6) applies.

### 2. Background, Timeliness, Need and Opportunity

Topological methods can already detect and quantify spatial organisation in multi-agent systems, but established results assume that organisation is cooperative or slowly evolving — biological aggregation, collective motion and flocking [11–13], and topological change-point detection [14]. These rely on persistent homology [1,2] and its statistical summaries [6,8,9], stable under small measurement error [7,8] and therefore comparable and averageable. Multi-scale methods exist for slowly evolving cooperative systems [10] but not for hierarchical competitive dynamics, where conventional approaches remain single-scale geometric descriptors [15,16]. Our pilot [17] recovers three stable connected-component regimes and two complementary loop regimes across 10 realisations, with the scales carrying largely independent information.

**Timeliness.** Three developments have converged. Multi-scale topology and statistical comparison tools have matured [4,6,8,9]. Scalable computation now supports rigorous analysis at population scale. Fully labelled competitive tracking data have reached that same scale. No statistical-topology treatment of competitive collective systems combines all three.

**Need and opportunity.** The gap is specific: no validated statistical-topology workflow exists for systems generated by continuous competitive interaction. Professional football is a well-observed platform on which to close it — all agents are continuously tracked within strict boundaries and domain experts can verify results — and the framework generalises to any bounded competitive system once interaction lengths are re-derived.

### 3. Impact, National Importance and Beneficiaries

**Mathematical impact.** The primary contribution is new statistical-topology theory for dependent competitive systems, extending current foundations [6,8] to sequential adversarial dynamics, with both results implemented in an open-source library. The primary beneficiaries — researchers in topology, statistics and complex systems — gain those foundations and the software to apply them.

**National importance.** UK groups lead internationally in statistical topology and stochastic geometry, but current methods [6,8] remain confined to static or exchangeable settings. This project develops UK capability at that frontier: statistical TDA and sequential inference for function-space-valued data.

**Economic and industry impact.** Co-developed with Swansea City AFC (SCAFC), the project delivers practitioner outputs unavailable from conventional geometry; secondary beneficiaries are sports analytics researchers and practitioners.

**Standard Grant pathway.** The T1/T2 foundations prepare a follow-on Standard Grant transferring the guarantees to spatial predator–prey dynamics, including tumour–immune competition with Co-I Powathil.

## Approach

### 4. Research Design and Objectives

**Project structure.** The PI (0.2 FTE) leads framework development, analysis and publication; two Co-Investigators (0.1 FTE combined) supply statistical and domain expertise. A Research Associate (Months 2–10) implements the pipeline and runs the full-season analysis — structured postdoctoral training in statistical topology, sequential inference and competitive-systems methodology. SCAFC provides tracking data and tactical labels under its StatsBomb agreement.

**Sample-size rationale.** A full Championship season supplies the replication the 10-realisation pilot cannot: 552 fixtures, ≈540 after pre-registered tracking-quality exclusions (R2). The unit of analysis is the match, with one focal team per fixture and the opponent as a covariate; the two teams in a fixture are not independent and are not counted separately. Approximately 32 matches per fully crossed covariate cell (venue × opponent strength × phase of play) give a 95% CI half-width of 0.025 for the tactical-scale loop-presence rate (pilot s.d. 0.072 [17]); 180 matches per formation detect Cohen's d ≥ 0.30 at 80% power (α = 0.05, BH-FDR). A borderline within-match pilot effect (p = 0.051) is the replication target.

**O1: Population-scale geometry (PI Months 1–9; RA Months 2–9).** O1 determines whether scale-specific summaries are stable enough to average at population scale, and whether distances between them distinguish competitive organisational states. It succeeds if all three hold:

- **Cutoff stability ≥ 0.80** — intraclass correlation of interaction lengths across a 20-match validation batch; below this, lengths are re-derived.
- **Mixing verified** — empirical autocovariance decay consistent with the summable-mixing condition T1 and T2 assume.
- **Discriminability** — separation of ≥3 tactical formations (p < 0.05, BH-corrected).

These are the hypotheses under which O2 proves T1 and T2.

**O2: Inference for dependent topological processes (PI and RA, Months 4–10).** O2 proves both theorems by functional analysis of landscape-valued time series and sequential change-point detection (§5). It succeeds if both are proved under the O1 conditions and detected change-points recover ≥70% of held-out annotated transitions within ±10 s (permutation p < 0.05).

### 5. Methodology

**Pipeline.** A containerised Python pipeline processes each match frame as a point cloud. Agents are partitioned using empirically derived interaction lengths validated against the pilot regimes (§2); failure triggers the O1 cutoff gate. Persistent homology is computed at each accepted scale using Ripser [20], GUDHI [21] and giotto-tda [22], and barcodes converted to persistence landscapes. Processing runs at 1 Hz. For O1, landscape distributions are compared across formations and covariate cells by landscape L² and Wasserstein distances, with discriminability tested by permutation under BH-FDR correction.

**T1.** On a bounded domain with fixed agent count, landscapes are uniformly bounded in the separable Hilbert space L² [8], so the mean path is the Bochner expectation and, by strict convexity, the unique Fréchet mean — uniqueness that fails for diagram-valued means [28]. The substance is the limit law: for a stationary, α-mixing landscape series with summable coefficients, the empirical mean path is consistent at n^(−1/2), with the long-run rather than the marginal covariance in the limit [24,25]. Dependence does not move the mean; it changes every variance built on it, licensing FPCA [18] on landscape trajectories and making the block bootstrap the correct calibration.

**T2.** The landscape map is Lipschitz from diagrams under the bottleneck distance into L², with constant C explicit in agent count and domain diameter; boundedness supplies the total-persistence condition [7,27]. For a functional CUSUM [19,26] on the landscape series, T2 bounds localisation error by |τ̂ − τ| = O_P(σ²/(Δ − 2Cε)²) for Δ > 2Cε, with Δ the size of the change in the mean landscape, σ² T1's long-run variance and ε the worst-case diagram perturbation. A transition is localisable only when it exceeds twice the measurement-induced perturbation, and block-bootstrap thresholds are that bound in operational form. FPCA scores are retained for interpretation; the projected variant needs an eigengap condition, checked in O1.

### 6. Feasibility and Risk Management

The project is feasible in twelve months: the method is pilot-validated (§2), so O2 begins from established conditions; data access is secured through the SCAFC agreement; and the computational budget is modest (§7). Three residual risks remain.

- **(R1) Scale transferability** — medium likelihood, high impact. Without transferable interaction lengths, averaging is not meaningful; the O1 cutoff gate is the mitigation.
- **(R2) Tactical label uncertainty** — medium likelihood, low impact. Dual-source verification, Cohen's κ and pre-registered exclusions mitigate this; labels affect O1 interpretation, not the theorems.
- **(R3) Landscape theory** — low likelihood, medium impact. If intractable, O2 falls back to the Wasserstein diagram comparison demonstrated in the pilot, and T1 uniqueness is not claimed.

### 7. Outcomes, Team and Resources

**Publications and software.** Acceptance of the methodology paper [17], a season-results paper, a football-analytics paper [23], a Zenodo library DOI and SCAFC practitioner outputs are the dated deliverables in Figure 1, forming the Month-12 evidence pack for the follow-on Standard Grant (§3).

**Team.** Based at Swansea University's Zienkiewicz Institute for Modelling, Data and AI, the project combines expertise not previously brought together in a single grant: statistical topology (PI), mathematical oncology (Co-I Powathil) and sport and exercise science (Co-I Kilduff), with SCAFC as the industry co-development setting — a new collaboration bridging mathematical, biological and sporting communities, and a natural fit for the scheme's remit. Prof Kilduff turns organisational-state distinctions into tactically meaningful categories; Prof Powathil advises on the T1 well-posedness argument. The oncology pathway is for the Standard Grant, not this award.

**Resources.** Full-season processing uses the PI's Supercomputing Wales allocation: approximately 1,600 of 5,000 available CPU-hours. The containerised pipeline runs on local machines and HPC clusters.
