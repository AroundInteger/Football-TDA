VISION
Quality and Mathematical Importance
Competitive spatial systems — collections of agents that coordinate within groups while competing between them in a bounded domain — are ubiquitous in applied mathematics: autonomous vehicle fleets sharing airspace, opposing crowd flows at transport hubs, predator–prey populations, and sports teams contesting territory. A distinguishing feature is that they operate simultaneously across multiple spatial scales: individual interactions (metres), small-group tactical structure (tens of metres), and system-wide organisation (tens of metres). Standard single-parameter persistent homology applied to such systems conflates topological features from these distinct levels into a single persistence diagram, making scale-specific attribution and tracking of scale dynamics over time impractical.
This challenge is recognised in the TDA community. Schindler and Barahona (2023) develop tools for analysing multiscale clusterings with persistent homology, directly addressing hierarchical decomposition but in cooperative, quasi-static settings. Multiparameter persistence theory (Botnan and Lesnick, 2022) provides algebraic machinery for simultaneous multi-scale analysis but faces computational barriers at the point-cloud scales relevant here. Our framework takes a complementary, domain-informed route: hierarchical clustering at validated cutoff distances decomposes the competitive point cloud by organisational level before computing standard single-parameter persistent homology at each level. This requires a second step — adaptive filtration — to ensure that the Vietoris–Rips threshold is appropriate for the post-clustering geometry at each scale. The mathematical novelty lies in (i) systematic, empirically validated scale identification for competitive systems, replacing heuristic filtration choices; (ii) adaptive filtration ensuring consistent H1 loop detection across all scales; and (iii) persistence landscape theory for competitive sequential diagrams, enabling rigorous detection of topological phase transitions.
Validated Foundation
Validation across 10 professional football matches (SkillCorner open broadcast tracking; 10 Hz; 1,500 uniformly sampled frames; 104,722 event–topology pairs) establishes three H0 analysis regimes — individual (2.98 m), tactical (12.0 m), team (30.0 m) — with cross-epoch stability scores of 0.88, 0.97, and 1.00. Two complementary H1 regimes are confirmed: individual-scale loops are near-universal (97.0% ± 1.5% frame presence across 10 matches) and transient; tactical-scale loops are rarer (19.3% ± 7.2%) but geometrically persistent. Scale complementarity is a mathematical property of the hierarchical decomposition — it partitions the filtration parameter space by construction — confirmed empirically (Spearman ρ = 0.254, 95% bootstrap CI [0.200, 0.314]). Topological features respond coherently to match dynamics across all 10 matches: on-ball engagements and quick breaks decrease persistence (p < 0.001), build-up phases increase it (p < 0.001), with effect signs consistent across event-window half-widths of ±0.5–5 s. The preliminary work is fully reproducible: all tracking data are from the SkillCorner public repository; the Python implementation will be open-source released alongside journal submission.
Timeliness
The Botnan and Lesnick (2022) survey signals theoretical maturity in multiparameter persistence; practical validated workflows for domain-informed decomposition remain underdeveloped. Simultaneously, professional tracking datasets have reached the scale — a Championship season exceeds 500 matches — where population-level topological statistics are for the first time computationally tractable. The 10-match open-data validation provides the methodological confidence to exploit this opportunity immediately.
Beneficiaries
Direct beneficiaries are the computational topology community (validated multi-scale workflows, open-source Python library) and sports analytics practitioners (interpretable formation metrics grounded in rigorous mathematics). Indirect beneficiaries include operators of any bounded competitive multi-agent system with tracking data: the domain-validated scale-identification approach transfers wherever empirically identifiable characteristic length scales exist, including autonomous vehicle platoon coordination, competitive crowd management at transport hubs, and ecological predator–prey monitoring.

APPROACH
Objectives
Building on the validated framework (Brown et al., in preparation; target journal submission prior to grant submission), four objectives are pursued over 12 months:
O1 — Population-level topological statistics: Scale to a full Championship season (~540 matches), establishing distributional properties of persistence diagrams across a competitive population. The mathematical question: do the stability scores, H1 presence rates, and complementarity metrics from 10-match open-data validation hold as population-level laws, or are they sensitive to competition context, tracking technology, or tactical diversity?
O2 — Topological fingerprinting of tactical systems: Develop Fréchet mean barcodes and inter-diagram distances in persistence space that distinguish formation classes, providing the first topologically grounded definition of formation identity across matches.
O3 — Temporal dynamics and phase transition classification: Extend persistence landscape theory (Bubenik, 2015; Chazal et al., 2014) to competitive sequential diagrams, treating each match as a path t ↦ λ_δ(t) in landscape space and developing CUSUM change-point detection for intra-match topological transitions.
O4 — Standard Grant evidence synthesis: Compile full-season results and landscape methods into a reproducible evidence base for a Standard Grant application. O1–O3 constitute standalone publishable contributions independently of this strategic output.
Methodology
The Python pipeline (Ripser/GUDHI/giotto-tda; <2 s per frame on Supercomputing Wales; containerised with Apptainer for reproducibility) processes player position data through GPS-aware hierarchical clustering at validated scales, restricted Vietoris–Rips filtration, and barcode computation. For O1, the pipeline runs across Championship matches available under the Swansea City AFC–StatsBomb data agreement [confirm: season coverage and match count]. Pre-registration of O2 hypotheses is deposited on OSF in Month 2 before full-season results are examined, ensuring confirmatory and exploratory analyses are clearly distinguished. For O3, landscape computation uses GUDHI's landscape module, with Fréchet mean estimation and CUSUM methods adapted from Chazal et al. (2014) to the competitive sequential setting.
Feasibility and Risk
Computation: ~540 matches × ~2,700 frames = ~1.46M frames. At <2 s per frame, ~3,000 CPU-hours are required; Supercomputing Wales allocation [confirm hours] comfortably accommodates this. The pipeline is embarrassingly parallel; batch testing on 50 matches in Month 1 validates stability before full-season execution.
Data access: The StatsBomb agreement is confirmed [confirm season/match count]. Contingency: a half-season analysis (~270 matches) remains sufficient for population-level distributional claims and is independently publishable if coverage is less than anticipated.
Technical: The H0 artifact from naive component counting has been resolved (GPS-aware clustering, validated). Single-linkage chaining at the tactical scale means reported H1 counts are conservative; future linkage comparison is deferred to the Standard Grant phase. The primary technical risk is that competitive sequential landscape theory may require non-trivial extension beyond Bubenik (2015); Wasserstein distance comparison provides a fallback if landscape methods are not fully developed within the timeline.

Monthly progress reviews against deliverables allow early identification of delay.
