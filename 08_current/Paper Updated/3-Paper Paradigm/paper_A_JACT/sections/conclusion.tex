\section{Conclusion}

This paper reports an empirical study of scale-attributable persistent
homology on competitive multi-agent point clouds, using ten professional
football matches as the measurement setting. Domain-informed clustering
and a data-driven truncation of the Vietoris--Rips parameter are
measurement choices, not new algebraic machinery; they made a stable
result possible on noisy broadcast tracking. Three $H_0$ regimes and two
$H_1$ regimes hold across independent matches and across wide ranges of
the cutoff and truncation percentile. The two $H_1$ scales carry
complementary rather than redundant information. The same pipeline
applies to other bounded competitive systems once interaction lengths
are re-derived.
