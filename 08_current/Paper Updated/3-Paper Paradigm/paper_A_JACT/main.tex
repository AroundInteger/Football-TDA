\title[Multi-scale persistent homology]{Multi-Scale Persistent Homology for Competitive Collective Systems}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Rowan} \sur{Brown}}\email{m.r.brown@swansea.ac.uk}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Department of Biomedical Engineering}, \orgname{Swansea University}, \orgaddress{\city{Swansea}, \country{United Kingdom}}}

\abstract{Competitive collective systems, including sports teams contesting a
pitch, organise at several named levels at once: individuals, small
groups, and a system-wide envelope. Persistent homology records
connected components ($H_0$) and loops ($H_1$) as a distance parameter
grows. On the full point cloud those levels mix in one diagram, so a
change cannot be assigned to a level. This paper asks whether the
homology can be made scale-attributable: each diagram belonging to one
organisational level.
The levels are named by how many groups they contain, not by an assumed
length. A cutoff sweep then recovers those distances from the
group-count curve. Hierarchical clustering at those recovered lengths
reduces the cloud to centroids, and Vietoris--Rips persistence is
computed on the centroids.
On ten professional football matches (broadcast tracking at 10~Hz,
positions in metres), the named group-count targets are met at
individual ($2.75$~m), tactical ($11.75$~m), and team ($23.0$~m).
A Vietoris--Rips loop needs at least four vertices.
At the team cutoff, four centroids occur in 141 of 436{,}648 frames;
a loop is impossible in the others, and none of those 141 frames
carries a loop. Loops appear at the individual level in
$96.5\%\pm 1.5\%$ of frames (mean bar length 1.886~m) and at the
tactical level in $18.8\%\pm 6.5\%$ (mean bar length 3.411~m). On the
primary match, every finite $H_1$ bar has a simple-cycle
representative, present at birth by construction, and the two levels
carry distinct information.
Applying the pipeline to another bounded competitive system requires
re-deriving the interaction lengths, which is not tested here.}

\keywords{Vietoris--Rips filtration, topological data analysis, spatial tracking, hierarchical clustering, team sports}
