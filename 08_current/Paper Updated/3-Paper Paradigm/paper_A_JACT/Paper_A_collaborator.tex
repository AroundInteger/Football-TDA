\textbf{What this paper is.}
An empirical study of whether persistent homology can be made
\emph{scale-attributable} on a real, high-frequency, noisy point cloud
from a competitive collective system.
Football is the measurement setting, not the audience.

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{What it is not.}
A football-analytics paper.
Event interpretation, geometric baselines, bilateral (home/away)
coupling, and predictive utility belong to a companion manuscript
(Paper~B, \emph{Journal of Sports Sciences}, in preparation).
Paper~A treats event correlation only as a construct-validity check
(``not noise'').

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Headline claim.}
Clustering at recovered interaction lengths, then a truncation of the
Vietoris--Rips parameter taken from the reduced cloud, produces stable
scale-attributable diagrams on broadcast tracking.
On ten professional matches: three $H_0$ regimes and two $H_1$ regimes;
the two $H_1$ levels carry distinct information; every examined $H_1$
feature has a geometric cycle proxy.

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Locked cutoffs (metres).}
Individual $2.75$; tactical $11.75$; team $23.0$
(cardinality inversion on all complete SkillCorner frames).
Operative tactical $H_1$ range: $[6,14]$\,m.

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Sample.}
Ten A-League matches, SkillCorner open broadcast tracking at $10$\,Hz.
Persistent homology on $150$ uniformly spaced complete frames per match
($1{,}500$ frames in total).
Primary match: SkillCorner ID~$1996435$, $43{,}531$ complete $22$-player
frames.

team-level $H_1$ needs a different representation (centroids collapse
to $k\in\{1,2\}$).

\begin{abstract}
Competitive collective systems, including sports teams contesting a
pitch, organise at several named levels at once: individuals, small
groups, and a system-wide envelope. Persistent homology records
connected components ($H_0$) and loops ($H_1$) as a distance parameter
grows. On the full point cloud those levels mix in one diagram, so a
change cannot be assigned to a level. This paper asks whether the
homology can be made scale-attributable: each diagram belonging to one
organisational level.
Positions are recorded in metres on a standard football pitch. The
levels are named by how many groups they contain, not by an assumed
length. A cutoff sweep then recovers those distances from the
group-count curve. Hierarchical clustering at those recovered lengths
decomposes the cloud, and persistent homology is computed on the
resulting centroids.
On ten professional matches (broadcast tracking at 10~Hz), three
$H_0$ regimes appear: individual (2.75~m), tactical (11.75~m), and
team (23.0~m). Loops appear at the first two levels, not the third.
Individual-level loops appear in $96.5\%\pm 1.5\%$ of frames (short
bar length in metres); tactical-level loops appear in
$18.8\%\pm 6.5\%$ (longer bar length). Every examined loop is
realised in space, and the two levels carry distinct information.
The same pipeline applies to other bounded competitive systems once
interaction lengths are re-derived.
\end{abstract}
