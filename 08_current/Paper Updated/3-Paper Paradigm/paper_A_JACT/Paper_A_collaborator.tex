\textbf{What this paper is.}
An empirical measurement study, not a new filtration theory.
It asks whether persistent homology can be made \emph{scale-attributable}
on a real, high-frequency, noisy point cloud.
Football is the measurement setting, not the audience.

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{What it is not.}
A football-analytics paper, and not a claim of new algebraic machinery.
Event interpretation, geometric baselines, bilateral (home/away)
coupling, and predictive utility belong to a companion manuscript
(Paper~B, \emph{Journal of Sports Sciences}, in preparation).
Paper~A treats event correlation only as a construct-validity check
(``not noise'').

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Headline claim.}
Clustering at validated interaction lengths, then a data-driven
truncation of the Vietoris--Rips parameter, produces stable
scale-attributable diagrams on broadcast tracking.
On ten professional matches: three $H_0$ regimes and two $H_1$ regimes;
the two $H_1$ scales carry distinct information; every examined $H_1$
feature has a geometric cycle proxy.

\vspace{0.4em}

Individual $2.98$; tactical $12.0$ (domain-informed, inside a

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Standard single-parameter Vietoris--Rips persistent homology applied to a
multi-agent point cloud mixes features from distinct organisational
levels in one diagram. This paper is an empirical study of whether
persistent homology can be made scale-attributable on a real,
high-frequency, noisy point cloud. We do not introduce a new filtration.
The point cloud is decomposed by hierarchical clustering at validated
interaction lengths, and Vietoris--Rips homology is computed on the
resulting centroids. Clustering changes the geometry, so a maximum
filtration scale suited to one cutoff returns no $H_1$ (loops) at
another. A data-driven truncation of the Vietoris--Rips parameter
removes that obstruction. On ten professional football matches
(broadcast tracking at 10~Hz), three stable $H_0$
(connected-component) regimes appear: individual (2.98~m), tactical
(12.0~m), and team (30.0~m). Two $H_1$ regimes appear with them:
individual-scale loops are frequent and transient
($97.0\%\pm1.5\%$ frame presence across matches); tactical-scale loops
are rarer but more persistent ($19.3\%\pm7.2\%$); team-scale $H_1$ is
absent a priori as a structural consequence of the clustering. Both
structures hold across matches and across wide ranges of the cutoff and
the truncation percentile. The two $H_1$ scales carry distinct rather
than redundant information (Spearman $\rho=0.264$ across 1{,}500 sampled
frames). Geometric cycle representatives are recovered for every $H_1$
feature examined. A minimal association with match events is a
construct-validity check against measurement noise. Football is the
measurement setting. The same pipeline applies to other bounded
competitive systems once interaction lengths are re-derived.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} persistent homology, multi-scale topology,
Vietoris--Rips filtration, multi-agent systems, spatial tracking data,
sports analytics
