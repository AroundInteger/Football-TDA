# Theoretical Grounding for TDA of Collatz Dynamics: A Literature Assessment

## TL;DR
- **There is no prior peer-reviewed work applying topological data analysis (persistent homology) to the Collatz conjecture or its sequences** — this is a genuine open niche, not an oversight; the closest legitimate machinery is persistent homology of dynamical orbits via Takens delay embedding, which nobody has yet pointed at Collatz.
- The three phenomena you observe map onto **well-established** theory: the n≡7(mod 8) enrichment is explained by Terras's parity-vector / 2-adic congruence structure (7 mod 8 is the residue class that forces two consecutive non-decreasing Syracuse steps and has the longest local stopping-time deferral), the H₁-deficit-strengthening-with-scale matches the known result that random/high-dimensional and ultrametric (2-adic, tree-like) data become **increasingly ultrametric and topologically trivial** in H₁ as scale/dimension grows, and the Collatz even/odd switch is formally a **2-state regime-switching dynamical system**, the same class of models used for predator–prey role reversal, which do have coexistence/stationary-distribution theorems.
- Your cited "Bradley (2009), 'Ultrametrics and p-adic analysis in persistent homology'" **does not exist under that title**; the correct references are Bradley (2017) "Finding Ultrametricity in Data using Topology" (Journal of Classification 34(1), 76–84) and Bradley (2010) "From image processing to topological modelling with p-adic numbers." The ratio(ℓ)=a+b/ℓ over prime coefficient fields is best framed not through the Hasse principle directly but through **multi-field persistent homology and torsion detection** (Boissonnat–Maria), which is where prime coefficient fields genuinely change Betti numbers.

## Key Findings

**1. TDA + Collatz: essentially virgin territory.** A thorough search returns no credible paper computing persistent homology, Betti numbers, or persistence diagrams of Collatz orbits or integer sequences. The only item pairing the terms is a self-published GitHub "proof" (Kobayashi, "ak-collatz-collapse") that is almost certainly crank/AI-generated pseudo-mathematics — the same framework claims to prove the ABC and Hodge conjectures with identical boilerplate — and uses "persistent homology" as terminology rather than computation. It should not be cited. The legitimate adjacent literature is (i) persistent homology of time-series/dynamical orbits via Takens embedding (Perea–Harer; and surveys such as Ravishanker–Chen 2021), and (ii) statistical-physics analyses of Collatz hailstone sequences — specifically M. G. E. da Luz, D. M. G. dos Santos, E. P. Raposo & G. M. Viswanathan, "Scale-free behavior in hailstone sequences generated by the Collatz map," *Phys. Rev. Research* 3, 013073 (published 22 January 2021), which reports "strong evidence of a scale-free power law for the Collatz map… consistent with geometric Brownian motion with drift, which is compatible with the validity of the Collatz conjecture" — but which does not use topology. Your project therefore sits in a real, defensible gap.

**2. n≡7 (mod 8) as a Collatz "boundary" class.** By Terras's theorem (1976), two positive integers share the same length-k parity vector iff they are congruent mod 2^k; equivalently every residue class mod 2^k determines the first k parities deterministically. Under the accelerated/Syracuse map T(n)=(3n+1)/2 for odd n, an integer with n≡3 (mod 4) (which includes 7 mod 8) does not drop below itself on the first step; 7 mod 8 specifically produces a run of consecutive "up"/odd steps before a division, so these integers have the largest local stopping-time deferral. Empirically the residue classes r=7,15,27,31 (mod 32) are exactly those documented to have stopping times exceeding 5 — i.e., 7 mod 8 is the "slowest-to-drop" boundary stratum. This is why 7 mod 8 is enriched near any threshold defined by "not yet dropped."

**3. Tao (2019), "almost all," and the exceptional set.** Tao proved: for any f with f(N)→∞, Col_min(N) < f(N) for almost all N in the sense of **logarithmic density** (not the stronger natural density). "Almost all" here means the exceptional set has logarithmic density zero — it is NOT known to be finite or empty. In Terence Tao, "Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values," *Forum of Mathematics, Pi* 10 (2022), e12 (arXiv:1909.03562), Remark 1.4, he states verbatim: "One could ask whether it is possible to sharpen the conclusion of Theorem 1.3 further, to assert that there is an absolute constant C₀ such that Col_min(N) ≤ C₀ for almost all N ∈ N+1. However, this question is likely to be almost as hard to settle as the full Collatz conjecture and out of reach of the methods of this paper." The exceptional set is characterized only negatively (density-zero); Tao's method — via Syracuse random variables, 3-adic distribution, and Fourier decay — cannot pin it down. Predecessors: Terras (Col_min(N)<N a.e.); Allouche (1979), who obtained density-one for any fixed θ > 3/2 − log₂3 ≈ 0.869; and Ivan Korec, "A density estimate for the 3x+1 problem," *Mathematica Slovaca* 44 (1994), Theorem 1, covering every real c > log₄3 = 0.79248125….

**4. Local-global / Hasse and the ratio(ℓ)=a+b/ℓ law.** The Hasse principle says a global (rational) solution exists iff local solutions exist over ℝ and every ℚ_p. The direct analogy to your ratio-over-prime-field law is imperfect: the Hasse principle is about existence of solutions, whereas ratio(ℓ) over coefficient fields ℤ/ℓ is about **rank (Betti number) dependence on the field**, which is governed by the Universal Coefficient Theorem and torsion. The genuinely relevant result is Boissonnat–Maria multi-field persistent homology: Betti numbers over ℤ/ℓ differ from the rational Betti number exactly when there is ℓ-torsion in integral homology, and computing across many primes lets one infer the prime divisors of torsion coefficients. A law of the form a+b/ℓ decaying in ℓ is the signature of torsion contributions that vanish for large primes — an adelic/"assemble across all primes" flavor, but the correct machinery is UCT + multi-field persistence, not the Hasse–Minkowski theorem.

**5. p-adic topology and persistent homology (Bradley).** Your cited title is incorrect. Bradley's actual relevant works: **Patrick E. Bradley, "Finding Ultrametricity in Data using Topology," *Journal of Classification* 34(1) (2017), 76–84, doi:10.1007/s00357-017-9228-8** — which links ultrametricity to TDA by using a quotient of connected components to maximal cliques in the Vietoris–Rips graph as an ultrametricity index; "On the Logistic Behaviour of the Topological Ultrametricity of Data" (2019); and "From image processing to topological modelling with p-adic numbers" (*p-Adic Numbers, Ultrametric Analysis and Applications* 2010) — the Bradley work that explicitly invokes persistence barcodes (Carlsson–Zomorodian). The real 2009 Bradley paper is **Bradley, P.E. (2009), "On p-adic Classification," *p-Adic Numbers, Ultrametric Analysis, and Applications* 1(4), 271–285, doi:10.1134/S2070046609040013**, which is about a p-adic modification of LBG clustering, not persistent homology. Broader context: dendrograms are ultrametric spaces / objects of p-adic geometry (Bruhat–Tits trees), and a very recent line ("Precision-Graded Cohomology and Arithmetic Persistence for Network Sheaves," arXiv:2511.00677, 2025) defines "arithmetic barcodes" over ℤ_p where the filtration is p-adic precision and bar length records p-adic valuation/torsion — directly relevant to a prime-field ratio law.

**6. Arithmetic topology (Mazur).** Mazur's analogy (unpublished 1963 notes, following conversations with Mumford) likens primes to knots via the embedding Spec(𝔽_p) ↪ Spec(ℤ) seen through étale topology: Spec(𝔽_p) behaves like a circle/knot, Spec(ℤ) like S³, number fields like 3-manifolds, the class group like H₁ of a 3-manifold. Developed by Kapranov, Reznikov, Morishita (book "Knots and Primes: An Introduction to Arithmetic Topology," Springer 2012). No prior work applies persistent homology within arithmetic topology — this connection appears novel, though it is analogy-level rather than a computational bridge.

**7. Switching dynamical systems and predator–prey role reversal.** The Collatz even/odd branch selection is a 2-state switching system. There is a mature theory: Kolmogorov/Lotka–Volterra systems under "telegraph noise" (2-state Markov switching) have theorems guaranteeing existence and uniqueness of a stationary distribution (Nguyen, Du, Yin; "Existence of stationary distributions for Kolmogorov systems of competitive type under telegraph noise," *J. Differential Equations* 257 (2014), 2078–2101). Strikingly, switching between two environments — even two in which the same species is favored — can produce coexistence (Benaïm–Lobry; Hening–Nguyen). Predator–prey role reversal (Barkai–McQuaid empirically) is modeled in **Faustino Sánchez-Garduño, Pedro Miramontes & Tatiana T. Marquez-Lago, "Role reversal in a predator–prey interaction," *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 1 (2014), 140186 (arXiv:1404.2685)**, whose model "exhibits excitable-like behaviour," reproducing the Barkai & McQuaid (1988) rock-lobster (*Jasus lalandii*) / whelk (*Burnupena papyracea*) reversal via a threshold control parameter switching between (i) classical oscillatory predator–prey dynamics and (ii) the former prey driving its predator to extinction — a 2-state possession/role switch directly analogous to the even/odd (contract/expand) switch in Collatz.

## Details

### On the three specific phenomena you asked to explain

**(a) n≡7 (mod 8) enrichment near the Collatz boundary.** The mechanism is purely 2-adic. Terras's parity-vector bijection (residue mod 2^k ↔ first k parities) means the "boundary" — integers that have not yet dropped below their start — is a union of residue classes mod 2^k. Among the mod-8 classes, odd n splits as 1,3,5,7. Classes ≡1 (mod 4) drop quickly (3n+1 is divisible by a higher power of 2); classes ≡3 (mod 4), and most acutely 7 (mod 8), generate consecutive Syracuse "up" steps and defer the drop longest. Hence a filtration or embedding that tracks "distance from dropping" will over-represent 7 mod 8 near its boundary, and this enrichment should sharpen as you refine the modulus (mod 16, 32, …), consistent with the documented r=7,15,27,31 (mod 32) long-stopping-time classes. The De Bruijn-graph structure of the Collatz map mod 2^k (Laarhoven–de Weger 2012) gives the combinatorial backbone: these graphs are isomorphic to binary De Bruijn graphs, and the 2-adic limit recovers the Bernstein–Lagarias conjugacy.

**(b) H₁ deficit strengthening with scale.** Two independent theoretical strands predict a vanishing/weakening H₁ (loop structure) as scale grows for arithmetic point clouds carrying 2-adic structure. First, ultrametricity: Murtagh's and Bradley's results show that as dimensionality/sparsity increases, finite metric data become increasingly ultrametric, and ultrametric spaces are tree-like (totally disconnected, hierarchically nested balls) — trees have trivial H₁. If your integer embedding inherits 2-adic/tree geometry, H₁ should be structurally suppressed and the suppression should intensify with scale (Bradley's "logistic behaviour of topological ultrametricity"). Second, the coefficient-field dependence: if the loops you do see are torsion-supported, they will appear or disappear depending on the coefficient field ℤ/ℓ (multi-field persistence), which is exactly the regime where a ratio(ℓ)=a+b/ℓ law would arise. The combination — genuine loops suppressed by ultrametricity, residual loops governed by ℓ-torsion — is the natural theoretical home for a scale-strengthening H₁ deficit.

**(c) Collatz switching ↔ predator–prey role-reversal switching.** Both are 2-state switching dynamical systems where the active "mode" flips a contraction/expansion (or predator/prey possession) role. Formally, the accelerated Collatz map alternates a contracting map (÷2) and an expanding-then-contracting map ((3n+1)/2), with the switch determined by parity — a deterministic analog of the Markov "telegraph" switch in stochastic predator–prey role-reversal models. The convergence theory you want — guarantee of a stationary distribution — exists on the stochastic side (telegraph-noise Kolmogorov systems, Nguyen–Du–Yin 2014) and on the Collatz side takes the form of Kontorovich–Sinai's 2-adic ergodicity result and Tao's Syracuse-random-variable equidistribution. The honest caveat: for the actual deterministic Collatz iteration, the Markov/random model is a heuristic — it proves the surrogate chain mixes, but the link to the deterministic orbit is precisely what remains unproven (this is why Collatz is open).

## Caveats
- The predator–prey and switching-system "convergence to a stationary distribution" theorems are **stochastic** results (Markov/telegraph switching). Collatz switching is **deterministic**; the analogy is structural and heuristic, and importing a stationary-distribution guarantee does not constitute a proof of Collatz convergence — the deterministic-vs-random gap is exactly the open problem (Kontorovich–Sinai, Tao).
- "Almost all" in Tao is logarithmic density, weaker than natural density; the exceptional set is only known to be density-zero and could in principle be infinite. Do not overstate it as "all but finitely many."
- The Mazur arithmetic-topology analogy is an analogy, not a functor delivering computable persistent homology; treat any H₁-of-integers ↔ class-group correspondence as suggestive, not rigorous, absent a concrete construction.
- The ratio(ℓ)=a+b/ℓ ↔ Hasse-principle link is, on current evidence, a loose metaphor. The rigorous mechanism for field-dependent Betti numbers is the Universal Coefficient Theorem and torsion (multi-field persistence); "adelic/local-global" language should be used only illustratively unless you construct an actual local-to-global obstruction.
- Some sources encountered (viXra, ResearchGate "proofs" of Collatz, the GitHub "collapse" repo) are non-peer-reviewed and unreliable; none of the load-bearing claims above rest on them.
