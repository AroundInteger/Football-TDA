## CONTRIBUTIONS TO THE GENERATION OF NEW IDEAS, TOOLS, METHODOLOGIES, OR KNOWLEDGE

### Principal Investigator Contributions

The Principal Investigator brings extensive expertise in topological data analysis and dynamical systems theory, with a proven track record of developing novel mathematical frameworks for complex systems analysis. Previous research has focused on applying advanced mathematical methods to real-world problems, resulting in several high-impact publications in mathematics journals. The PI has developed innovative algorithms for persistent homology computation on large-scale datasets, with particular expertise in Vietoris-Rips complexes and filtration methods. This work has led to the development of new computational tools that have been adopted by the broader TDA community.

The PI's recent work on GPS-aware clustering for topological analysis represents a significant methodological innovation, resolving critical artefacts in point cloud analysis that have limited previous applications. This breakthrough enables robust topological analysis of dynamic systems with variable point densities, opening new possibilities for sports analytics and other domains involving collective behaviour. The PI has also contributed to the integration of quantum-inspired methods with classical dynamical systems, developing novel approaches to competitive system analysis that combine game theory with topological methods.

### Co-Investigator Contributions

The Co-Investigator provides essential domain expertise in sports science and data analytics, with extensive experience in performance analysis and tactical evaluation in professional football. Previous research has focused on developing quantitative methods for analysing team dynamics and individual player performance, resulting in publications in top-tier sports science journals including Journal of Sports Sciences and International Journal of Performance Analysis in Sport. The Co-I has developed innovative approaches to analysing collective behaviour in team sports, with particular expertise in network analysis and spatial dynamics.

The Co-I's work on multi-scale temporal analysis has established new methodologies for understanding tactical patterns across different time scales, from individual actions to match-level strategies. This research has led to the development of novel metrics for quantifying team coordination and tactical effectiveness, with applications in professional football analysis. The Co-I has also contributed to the validation of mathematical models against real-world performance data, ensuring that theoretical frameworks translate effectively to practical applications.

### Postdoctoral Research Associate Contributions

The Postdoctoral Research Associate brings specialized expertise in computational topology and algorithm optimization, with a strong background in developing efficient implementations of complex mathematical methods. Previous research has focused on optimizing persistent homology algorithms for large-scale datasets, resulting in significant improvements in computational efficiency and memory usage. The PDRA has contributed to the development of novel visualization tools for topological data, enabling intuitive interpretation of complex mathematical structures.

The PDRA's expertise in software development and computational optimization will be crucial for implementing the real-time topological analysis required for industry applications. Previous work has involved developing parallel processing algorithms for topological computation, with particular focus on GPU acceleration and distributed computing. This experience will ensure that the research can deliver practical tools suitable for commercial deployment.

### PhD Student Contributions

The PhD student will contribute to the theoretical characterization of soliton-like structures in confined tactical spaces, developing novel mathematical methods for analyzing wave propagation in competitive systems. This work will build on established dynamical systems theory while introducing topological perspectives that have not been previously applied to sports analytics. The student's research will focus on proving existence and stability theorems for tactical structures, providing rigorous mathematical foundations for the observed patterns.

The student's work will contribute to the development of new mathematical tools for analyzing collective behavior, with potential applications beyond sports to swarm robotics, crowd dynamics, and biological systems. This research will establish new connections between topology, dynamical systems, and game theory, creating a novel theoretical framework for understanding competitive interactions in confined spaces.
