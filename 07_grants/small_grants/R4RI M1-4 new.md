**Module 1 – Contributions to the Generation of New Ideas, Tools, and Methodologies**

**New ideas and methodologies:**\
This research applies multi-scale persistent homology to competitive spatial systems — an area where existing TDA methods operate at single scales. The validated framework (Brown et al., 2026, ArXiv preprint) introduces domain-informed hierarchical clustering for scale decomposition and adaptive filtration for consistent H1 detection across organisational levels. Validated across 11 professional football matches, the methodology enables spatial pattern analysis in hierarchical, competitive, time-evolving systems.

**Tools and key outputs:**\
The project delivers a computational framework implemented in Python, integrating established TDA libraries (Ripser, GUDHI, giotto-tda) with multi-scale functionality. Designed for efficiency (<2s per frame), the framework supports full-season analysis on HPC infrastructure. Key outputs include Paper 1 (methodology, ArXiv preprint, journal submission during grant), Paper 2 (cross-domain application, in preparation), and Paper 3 (full-season results and persistence landscapes, produced during grant). The work generates open-source software and curated datasets to support reproducibility.

**Communication of ideas and results:**\
Ideas and results are communicated through planned publications, conference presentations, and open-source releases; ensuring accessibility for both academic and applied communities.

**Skills acquired:**\
Through this research, the team has strengthened expertise in advanced mathematical modelling, computational implementation, and large-scale data processing. These skills, combined with a commitment to open dissemination, ensure that the project contributes not only new knowledge but also practical tools for the wider research community.
