# Team Member Skillsets and Benefits

**Principal Investigator (Dr Rowan Brown, Biomedical Engineering)**: Dr Brown brings specialist expertise in topological data analysis and computational modelling, having developed the multi-scale TDA framework with validated topological scales (individual: 3m, tactical: 12m, team: 30m) and adaptive filtration methods that enable robust pattern detection across hierarchical spatial scales. As project lead, Dr Brown provides strategic direction, develops and validates the initial processing pipeline on 15-20 games (Months 1-3), supervises the Research Associate, coordinates full-season analysis across all 540 games (Months 10-12), manages industry partnerships (Genius Sports, Borussia Dortmund, championship clubs), and leads manuscript preparation and Standard Grant development, ensuring the project delivers rigorous mathematical frameworks suitable for top-tier publications while establishing pathways for broader impact.

**Professor Liam Kilduff (Sport and Exercise Sciences)**: Professor Kilduff brings extensive expertise in sports science and performance analysis, providing critical domain knowledge for interpreting topological patterns within professional football contexts, validating analytical outputs against established tactical frameworks, and translating research findings into practical applications that resonate with sports industry stakeholders.

**Professor Gibin Powathil (Mathematics)**: Professor Powathil contributes essential applied mathematics expertise spanning computational methods and statistical analysis, providing TDA methodological guidance, rigorous statistical validation of multi-scale patterns, and theoretical framework development that ensures mathematical rigour suitable for top-tier publications in computational topology.

**Research Associate**: The Research Associate brings strong computational and analytical expertise in Python programming, TDA libraries (ripser, GUDHI, giotto-tda), parallel processing, and statistical analysis, enabling efficient implementation of the concurrent processing pipeline, large-scale multi-match validation across the full Championship season (~520-525 games, ~77,000-78,000 frame analyses), and development of pattern formation and temporal evolution methods that are essential for achieving the project's research objectives within the 12-month timeframe. The Research Associate will actively contribute to manuscript development as a co-author, participating in writing, data analysis, and figure preparation, and will be involved in grant development activities including the Standard Grant application preparation, gaining valuable hands-on experience in research proposal development and academic publication processes.
